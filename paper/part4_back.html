Appendix D. Implementation notes on the mechanisms

Feasibility: a vehicle can carry an item if the item's interface standard is in the vehicle's accepted set and the item's mass does not exceed the vehicle's capacity. Teams: for an item heavier than every free vehicle, candidate vehicles are those that are free, team-capable and compatible, sorted by capacity; the first pair or triple whose capacities sum to at least the item's mass is chosen. Routing within a bundle: all permutations of up to four distinct destinations are evaluated on slope-weighted distance from the pad. Insertion test: an item joins a bundle if its marginal duration is below 80% of the duration of a separate single-item job for the same vehicle. Pool cap: when more than 40 items are waiting, one auction call considers the 40 most urgent and oldest; remaining items are considered at the next event. Anticipation: a landing within 48 h whose pad has no present or inbound vehicle triggers a staging move by the nearest unused free vehicle, timed to arrive one hour before the earliest possible item release, unless the move would be caught by the night. Payments: each job is paid its variable cost (Section 3.5) and credited to the vehicle owners in equal shares for teams. Batched assignment uses a minimum-cost assignment (SciPy's linear_sum_assignment when available, otherwise a shortest-augmenting-path Hungarian implementation verified against SciPy on 300 random matrices) with cost = single-item job duration − 0.05 × waiting age; infeasible pairs cost 106. In-house carrier assignment: owners without vehicles are mapped round-robin to carriers in sorted order at the start of the run.
